# Rerack

**Know what to move, what to mark down, and what to save before the season ends.**

Rerack is a weekly decision tool for owners of multi-store clothing and footwear chains. Upload each store's stock CSV, and Rerack turns it into a short, ranked list of transfers and markdowns. Every suggestion shows the numbers behind it, and the owner approves, edits or rejects each one. Nothing is moved or repriced automatically.

> **Code does every number. The AI only ranks and explains.** Stock cover, sell-through and ageing are plain arithmetic in `lib/`. The AI reads the owner's free-text note, orders the candidates code already found, and writes a one-line reason. It never calculates and never invents a candidate.

Built as a hackathon MVP. The demo is a **fictional** chain, "Northstar Apparel" (five Bengaluru stores, 297 stock lines), generated by code and sent through exactly the same checks as a real upload.

---

## The MVP scope (source of truth)

**Job to be done.** *When* my stores' weekly sales and stock reports sit in separate files, *I want to* upload them and get a short ranked list of what to move or mark down, *so I can* approve the right actions in minutes and stop ageing stock reaching the end-of-season sale. **User:** the owner of a 3-30 store chain, mainly in India, who approves from a phone.

**In scope:** upload one CSV per store plus a free-text note; code calculates days since receipt, stock cover and sell-through and finds transfer and markdown candidates; the AI ranks the top 10 with a reason each; a phone-friendly approve / edit / reject flow with a time-stamped log.

**Deliberately not built** (the scope doc's "mention, don't build" list): automatic nightly pull from billing software, AI matching of inconsistent item names, WhatsApp/email notes to store managers, a price-change export, tracking whether actions worked. The app never claims any of these. Because the CSV template has no price or cost columns, Rerack shows **no rupee values and no margin figures**, and says why.

### The 3 tests

| # | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upload the store CSVs plus a note, press Process, and within 60 seconds see a ranked top-10 with the numbers and a reason for each | 2 to 3 seconds measured with the demo data |
| 2 | On a phone-width screen, approve or reject an action and see it time-stamped in a log that survives a refresh | Verified at 375 px with real taps |
| 3 | Bad input (empty file, wrong type, missing column, non-number, bad date, fewer than 2 stores) gives a plain message naming the file and problem, with no list and no AI call | 7 cases verified; bad input returns in about 20 ms on the server |

## The screens

| Screen | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Six calculated KPIs, stock held against sales by store, ageing distribution, risk by product, the top 5 priorities, and "selling well here, stuck there" gaps. An empty state offers the demo or an upload. |
| **Action Centre** | The ranked list (capped at 10). Each card shows the action, the key numbers, the reason (labelled AI or rules), an **evidence panel** (per-store figures, the step-by-step calculation, and what the data can't tell you) and **Approve / Edit / Reject**. Transfers then wait for a "stock verified" tick and offer a copyable transfer note that is clearly not sent anywhere. |
| **Inventory** | Every store, item, size and colour line with search, filters (store, product, size, colour, age, risk), sortable columns, pagination and a detail view. |
| **Stores** | Per-store summaries, a product-by-store matrix (sold, on hand, or held 60+ days), ageing by store, and variant-level gaps between stores. |
| **Data Imports** | Drag-and-drop or browse, editable store names, a live per-file preview and validation, duplicate-file and repeated-row warnings, a downloadable CSV template, notes with quick-add chips, a review step, and a processing summary. |
| **Activity & Reports** | Files imported, data-quality issues, actions proposed, approvals and rejections, the biggest at-risk stock, and a time-stamped log of every import and decision. It reports no savings or outcomes, because none are tracked. |
| **Rules** | The thresholds behind every flag, as plain editable numbers, plus the rules that never change. |

Transfers and markdowns are views inside the Action Centre (filter tabs), not separate pages, so there is one place to decide.

## Current workflow → future workflow

**Today:** record sales and stock per store in billing software or Excel → export each store's report → **merge the reports by hand** → **decide by eye what to discount or move** → whatever is left is cleared at deep discounts in the end-of-season sale.

**With Rerack:** upload one CSV per store → stores merge automatically by item, size and colour → code flags ageing stock and finds transfers and markdowns → the owner reviews a short ranked list with evidence and approves, edits or rejects → the season-end sale remains, with less stock reaching it.

## Architecture: Input → AI → Logic → Action → Output

```
INPUT            LOGIC (before)         AI                    LOGIC (after)           ACTION             OUTPUT
store CSVs  →    validate files    →    rank the candidates → check the answer →      owner approves,  → ranked cards with
+ owner's note   merge by style         write one-line        drop unknown ids        edits or rejects    evidence, the "left
                 ageing metrics         reasons               flag odd numbers        (time-stamped)      out" list, the log,
                 transfer + markdown    read the note         keep both types         transfers verified  and every dashboard
                 rules                                        fall back to rules
```

Logic runs **twice, before and after the AI**: the AI can only rank candidates that code has already found, and its answer must pass code checks.

| Stage | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Input | CSV validation (columns, whole numbers, dates, duplicates), merge by style | `lib/validate.ts`, `lib/metrics.ts` |
| Logic (before) | Days since receipt, weekly sales rate, stock cover, sell-through, risk classification, transfer and markdown candidates with a step-by-step calculation and a list of limitations | `lib/candidates.ts`, `lib/risk.ts`, `lib/config.ts` |
| AI | One call that returns a ranking, a reason per action, and what it left out because of the note, in a fixed JSON schema | `lib/rank.ts`, `lib/prompt.ts` |
| Logic (after) | Drop unknown or duplicate ids, flag any reason quoting a number code didn't produce, keep at least 4 transfers and 4 markdowns when available, and fall back to the rule-based order if the AI is down | `lib/rank.ts` |
| Action | Approve with a confirmation, edit the quantity or markdown with a live preview, reject with a reason, verify stock for transfers, undo | `components/ActionCard.tsx`, `lib/appState.ts` |
| Output | Dashboards, tables, charts and an audit log, all derived from the same rows so every total agrees | `lib/derive.ts`, `app/` |

**How the numbers work:** weekly sales = units sold ÷ the number of weeks in the **sales window** (see below). Stock cover = on hand ÷ weekly sales; with no sales it shows "No sales" and is never divided by zero. Sell-through = sold ÷ (sold + on hand). Days since receipt comes from the `date_received` column and may not equal the true age of the stock.

## Where AI is used, and what stays human

**AI:** reading the owner's note ("Diwali in 3 weeks", "Store B closed", "don't discount the wedding range"), ranking the candidates, and writing a one-line reason. A rule can't read free text. With an empty note the AI adds little beyond wording the reason.

**Plain code:** reading files, every number, the rules that find candidates, validating the AI's answer. The model and rules are configurable: thresholds on the Rules page, the model in `lib/config.ts`.

**Always human:** which files to upload and what to say in the note; approving, editing or rejecting every action; counting the physical stock before a transfer; entering any price change in the billing software.

## Failure cases, and what the screen does

| What goes wrong | What the user sees |
|---|---|
| **Bad file** (empty, not a CSV, missing column, non-number, bad date, fewer than 2 stores) | A plain message naming the file, row and problem. Processing stays blocked and the AI is not called. |
| **Duplicate file or repeated rows** | A warning, not a block. Repeated rows are added together. |
| **The AI invents or alters a number** | Every number comes from code. A reason quoting a number code didn't produce gets a red ⚠ tag. |
| **The AI pairs real numbers wrongly** | The ⚠ check **cannot** catch this, because every number is real. The evidence panel shows the truth. The prompt forbids it, but it is a known limit. |
| **The AI misreads the note and drops an action** | It appears under "Left out by the AI" with its reason, and can still be reviewed and approved. |
| **The AI ranks differently from the rules** | Each card shows "AI rank 2 · rules rank 5". |
| **The AI is down, slow, out of credit or rate-limited** | The rule-based list still appears with a banner saying why. |
| **Wrong stock data** | Rerack is only as good as the data it receives. Every transfer needs a verified physical count before dispatch. |
| **No price or cost in the data** | No rupee or margin figures are shown, and the app says so. |

## Run it locally

You need Node.js (LTS) and an [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai) API key.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/mayankagrawal-dot/rerack.git
cd rerack
npm install
```

Create `.env.local` in the project root (it is git-ignored, so your key is never committed):

```
OPENROUTER_API_KEY=sk-or-v1-your-key-here
```

```bash
npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:3000 and press **Explore the demo**, or go to **Data Imports** and drop in the files from `sample-data/` (five store files). The `bad-*` files show how bad input is handled. Without a key the app still works: you get the rule-based list and a banner saying the AI ranking is unavailable.

To deploy on Vercel, import the repo and add `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` under Project Settings, Environment Variables.

## CSV template

`item, size, colour, date_received, units_on_hand, units_sold`: one file per store, whole numbers 0 or more, dates as `2026-08-20` or `20/08/2026`. The file name suggests the store name, which you can change on the import screen. The older column names `units_sold_4w` and `units_sold_last_4_weeks` are still accepted.

## The sales window

The `units_sold` column is one total per line, so it covers some period of time. **You tell Rerack which dates that period is**, using the "Sales window" button on the first page (or step 2 of Data Imports): quick choices for the last 2, 4, 6, 8 or 12 weeks, or custom from and to dates (7 to 366 days, ending no later than today).

What this does, and what it can't do:

- It changes the weekly sales rate (`units sold ÷ weeks in the window`) and everything built on it: stock cover, which lines are flagged, which transfers and markdowns are suggested and by how much, the evidence on every card, and the wording the AI is told to use.
- Changing it on data you have already loaded re-runs the analysis (about 3 seconds). Because the suggested actions can change, **it clears the decisions you recorded**, and says so before you confirm. The change is written to the activity log.
- It **cannot re-cut sales for a different period**: the files hold one total per line, with no daily detail. Pick the dates that match how you exported the files. If you choose a different period from the one the files really cover, the weekly rates will be wrong in proportion.
- The default is the last 4 weeks. Under two weeks gets a warning, because a few busy days can swing a short window.

## Chart dates

The charts on the **Overview** and **Stores** pages (and the product-by-store matrix) have a "Chart dates" control. It limits them to stock **received** between two dates, using the `date_received` column: quick choices that match the ageing chart (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, over 90 days) or custom from and to dates, with either end optional. The choice is shared between the two pages, and a line under the control says how many lines and units the charts are showing.

- Only the charts follow it. The KPI cards at the top and the store summary cards always cover **all** stock, so the headline numbers never change under you.
- It filters by **received date**, not by sales date, because that is the only date on every line. Sales have no dates of their own (one total per line); they always cover the sales window.
- A store with no stock in the chosen range stays on the charts at zero instead of disappearing.
